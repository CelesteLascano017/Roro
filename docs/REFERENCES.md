# RORO — Visual References

## 1. Visual Direction

Roro is a 2D top-down pixel-art game with a psychological thriller atmosphere.

The visual style should combine the readability of classic RPG environments with a darker, more unsettling atmosphere.

The apartment should feel like a believable, lived-in space rather than a generic dungeon or fantasy environment.

## 2. Main Visual Reference: Top-Down RPG

The first two visual references shared for the project establish the desired gameplay perspective.

Characteristics to preserve:

- Top-down perspective.
- Clearly recognizable rooms and corridors.
- A character visible within the environment.
- Furniture and objects that can be distinguished at the game's resolution.
- Consistent pixel-art proportions.
- A camera that allows the player to understand the apartment layout.

These references are about perspective and spatial readability, not about copying another game's artwork.

## 3. Dialogue Visual Reference

The third reference establishes the presentation style for interviews.

Elements to consider:

- Character portrait.
- Dialogue box.
- Character name.
- Text displayed clearly.
- Consistent visual composition.

The dialogue interface should feel separate from the top-down exploration screen.

Dialogues are fixed, linear, and non-skippable.

## 4. Character Design

### Roro

- Must be recognizable from a top-down perspective.
- Needs directional movement animations.
- Must remain visually distinguishable from the detective.
- Should fit the proportions and pixel scale of the environment.

### Detective

- Must be recognizable as a police detective.
- Needs directional movement animations.
- Should remain visually distinguishable from Roro.
- Must be readable while moving through rooms and corridors.

## 5. Environment Design

The playable environment is a single apartment.

The environment should include:

- Connected rooms and corridors.
- Walls and doors.
- Furniture.
- Interactive objects.
- Places where evidence can be hidden.
- Clear visual boundaries for collision detection.

Furniture and interactive objects should be visually distinguishable.

## 6. Atmosphere and Color

The intended atmosphere is tense, quiet, and unsettling.

Visual decisions should support:

- A sense of confinement.
- Limited visibility.
- Contrast between illuminated and obscured areas.
- Clear readability of important objects.
- A coherent palette across all rooms.

Avoid excessive visual effects that make navigation or interaction difficult.

## 7. Difficulty and Visibility

The visual presentation must support three visibility modes:

- **Easy:** The complete apartment map and detective location are visible.
- **Normal:** Only the current room is visible.
- **Hard:** Visibility is limited to a radius around Roro.

The visibility system should obscure information without making the environment impossible to navigate.

## 8. Asset Consistency

All selected assets should be checked for:

- Pixel dimensions.
- Perspective.
- Color palette.
- Character scale.
- Animation frame size.
- License and permitted use.

Avoid mixing assets with noticeably different perspectives or pixel-art styles.

## 9. Artistic Restrictions

- Do not use AI-generated images.
- Do not copy complete scenes or characters from other games.
- Do not prioritize decorative detail over gameplay readability.
- Do not use assets that conflict with the top-down perspective.
- Prefer a consistent visual style over a large quantity of unrelated assets.

## 10. Final Art Direction

The final result should feel like a small, carefully constructed top-down RPG environment transformed into a psychological thriller.

The apartment, characters, objects, lighting, and dialogue interface must feel like parts of the same game.
